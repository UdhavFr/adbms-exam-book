Source: ADBMS_MasterNotes.docx — folder `app_how-to-write-a-20-mark-answer`

## How to write a 20-mark answer
_(p. 1)_
- Start with a 5–7 line introduction defining the concept and explaining its importance.
- Draw a labelled diagram or flow whenever the topic has an architecture, process, hierarchy, or algorithm.
- Explain every major component under separate headings. Do not compress all concepts into one paragraph.
- Give at least one practical example, preferably with a table, relation, transaction, query, or scenario.
- Add advantages, limitations, applications, or comparison points wherever relevant.
- Finish with a 3–4 line conclusion connecting the topic to database correctness, performance, scalability, or reliability.
A strong 20-mark answer is not simply a collection of definitions. It should show the examiner that you understand WHAT the concept is, WHY it is required, HOW it works, and WHERE it is useful.
